Short responses and litanies are usually 95–100% right. The hard cases are long melismas, where one syllable is sung for 30–70 seconds, and recordings that don't match the text (a different patriarch's name, a spoken introduction).

How it works

  1. Text: the hymn from tasbeha.org, where every Coptic word comes with its English-letter pronunciation (Greco-Bohairic).
  2. Sound: Meta's MMS speech model (trained on 1,100+ languages), mixed 50/50 with a copy fine-tuned on 108 minutes of hand-timed Coptic chant, hears which letters are being sung every 20 ms.
  3. Alignment: forced alignment places the known words in order on the recording, skipping audio that isn't in the text and lines that aren't sung, and letting a vowel be held through a melisma.

Also: reading Coptic manuscripts

The same project trained an OCR model for Sahidic Coptic manuscripts and print (line finder + ResNet-50/BiLSTM reader + Coptic language model), evaluated on the SCAM benchmark (character error rate, lower is better):
